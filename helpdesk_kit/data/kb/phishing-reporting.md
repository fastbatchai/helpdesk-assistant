# Phishing Reporting

Phishing messages try to get you to click a link, open an attachment or hand over information. They often claim to come from a manager, a carrier or a customer, and they often push you to hurry.

## Warning signs

- A sender's address that does not match the name displayed
- Urgent demands, threats or requests to keep something secret
- Unexpected attachments, invoices or shipping notices
- A sign-in page you reached from a link in a message
- Requests to change bank details or to buy gift cards

## You received something suspicious but did not click

1. Do not click on any links or open any attachments.
2. Press the Report Phish button in Outlook. On a phone, forward the message to phish@northgate.example.
3. The message is removed from your mailbox. No further action is needed.

IT Security reviews reports within one business day. You will not always hear back, but every report helps protect your colleagues.

## You clicked, opened or entered something

Act right away. Being quick matters far more than being certain, and nobody will be blamed for reporting honestly.

1. Phone the help desk on extension 4357. Do not just open a ticket. Outside help-desk hours, follow the voice prompts for the on-call engineer.
2. If you entered a password, reset it straight away at password.northgate.example, using a different device if you can.
3. If you opened an attachment, disconnect from the network but leave the laptop switched on, so that it can be examined.
4. Deny any MFA requests you did not start.

These cases are treated as at least P2 security incidents and are handled by an engineer. See Incident Severity Levels.

## Calls and text messages

Criminals also use phones. The help desk will never ask you for a password or MFA code. If in doubt, hang up and call extension 4357 yourself.
