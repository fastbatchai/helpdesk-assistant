# On-Call Escalation

This explains who handles an incident and how it moves between teams.

## The three support levels

**Level 1: help desk.** Takes all incoming calls and tickets, sets the priority and solves common problems. Staffed Monday to Friday, 07:00 to 19:00 CET.

**Level 2: specialists.** The network, systems, applications and security teams. They take issues Level 1 cannot solve, and during business hours they respond within the target for the priority.

**Level 3: vendors and senior engineers.** Called in by Level 2 for product faults or major design problems.

## Outside business hours

Only P1 incidents are handled outside help-desk hours. To report one, phone extension 4357 from an internal phone, or the on-call number on the back of your badge. Follow the voice prompts. The on-call engineer calls you back within 15 minutes.

Anything lower than P1 waits until the next business day. Report it through the portal, so that it is in the queue when the help desk opens.

## How a P1 escalates

1. **0 minutes:** Level 1 or the on-call engineer confirms the impact and declares a P1.
2. **15 minutes:** the on-call engineer starts work and opens an incident channel in Teams.
3. **30 minutes:** if the cause is unknown, the duty manager is paged and takes over communication.
4. **1 hour:** the duty manager updates the affected sites, and hourly from then on.
5. **4 hours:** if unresolved, the duty manager informs the Head of IT, who decides on any vendor or business escalation.

## Security incidents

Confirmed or suspected breaches go straight to the security on-call engineer, whatever the time. Only they may decide to isolate systems or contact outside parties.

## After the incident

Each P1 gets a written review within five business days. It covers the timeline, cause and follow-up actions, without assigning blame.

## If you feel your issue is stuck

Ask the help desk to escalate it, quoting your ticket number. They will review the priority and tell you who owns it.
