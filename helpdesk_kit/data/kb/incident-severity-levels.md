# Incident Severity Levels

IT uses four priority levels. The priority decides how fast we respond and who gets involved. When you report an issue, tell us who is affected and whether any work has stopped, because the engineer uses this to set the priority.

## P1: critical

A site, or a service that the business depends on, is down, and there is no workaround. Examples:

- a whole office has lost its network
- the warehouse management system is down and shipping has stopped
- a confirmed security breach or ransomware
- company-wide email or sign-in failure

First response in 15 minutes, resolution target of 4 hours, on call around the clock. Always phone. Do not use the portal for a P1.

## P2: high

A team is blocked, or there is a security risk that is not yet confirmed as a breach. Examples:

- one department cannot reach a key system
- a user entered their password on a phishing page
- a lost or stolen laptop or phone
- a critical system is badly degraded

First response within 1 business hour, resolution target of 1 business day. Phone during help-desk hours.

## P3: medium

One person is affected and has a workaround, or something is failing without stopping work. Examples:

- a single laptop is running slowly
- VPN keeps disconnecting for one user
- a printer is not working, with another nearby

First response within 4 business hours, resolution target of 3 business days.

## P4: low

Questions, minor requests and planned changes. Examples:

- how-to questions
- a new headset
- a software request

First response within 1 business day, resolution target of 10 business days.

## Changing a priority

An engineer can raise or lower a priority as more becomes known. If the impact grows, tell us, and we will reassess it. Senior staff do not get a higher priority because of their title, only because of the business impact.

To see how a P1 or P2 incident moves through the team, read On-Call Escalation.
